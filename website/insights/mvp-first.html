Why we start with an MVP

The first release should prove one journey for one user. Everything else is a later conversation — not a silent addition to the same quote.

Teams often arrive with a full product in mind: every role, every integration, a store listing, and a date. That is a programme, not a first release. We ask who the first user is, then cut until a named journey can ship.

What belongs in the first cut

Auth if you need it, the workflow that creates value, and enough operations to support that workflow. Not the marketplace’s second side, not every report, not a design system for screens that do not exist yet.

What we move to phase two

When the budget is tight, we do not discount the same scope. We move features that do not serve the first user. That is how Harvest launched a two-sided MVP in 16 weeks without pretending the whole market was in version one.
