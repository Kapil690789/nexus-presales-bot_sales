## You own what we build

On completion of payment for the work, all intellectual property in the deliverables is
yours: source code, design files, documentation, and the architecture. Assigned outright,
not licensed to you.

There is no clause that keeps a component of your product dependent on something we own.
If we build it for you as part of the engagement, it is yours.

## The two exceptions, stated plainly

**Open-source dependencies.** Every product uses third-party libraries, and those keep
their own licences. We stick to permissive licences — MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD — and we do
not introduce a copyleft dependency into your product without telling you what it means.
The dependency list and its licences come with handover.

**Our pre-existing tooling.** We bring internal scaffolding: project templates, CI
configuration, utility libraries. Where any of it ends up in your codebase, you get a
perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to use, modify, and distribute it as part of
your product. You will never need our permission to change your own software, and you can
take it to another supplier without asking us. What we retain is the right to keep using
our own generic tooling on other projects, which is not something you are buying.

## Confidentiality

Everything you share is confidential: your plans, your numbers, your documents, and the
fact of the conversation itself. We will sign your NDA — send it over, we do not insist on
our own template — and confidentiality obligations survive the end of the engagement
rather than expiring with it.

Practically, that means your material is discussed only within the team working on your
project, is not shown to other clients or used as an example without written permission,
and is deleted or returned on request.

## Using your project as a reference

We ask, in writing, and the default is no.

If you would rather we did not name you, we do not name you — not on the website, not in
a proposal, not in a sales conversation. Some clients allow an anonymised description
("a health network in the Midwest"), some allow the name, and some allow nothing. All
three are fine and none of them affect what you pay.

Testimonials are approved by the person quoted before they are published, and the approval
is recorded. In this assistant, a case study naming a client who has not agreed to be
named is held back until a visitor has accepted the confidentiality notice.

## Non-compete and exclusivity

We do not offer sector exclusivity, and we will tell you if we are working with a direct
competitor when the question is relevant. What protects you is the confidentiality
obligation and the fact that project teams do not overlap on competing products — not a
promise to decline an entire industry, which no consultancy of our size could honestly
make.

## What we take with us

Skill and general knowledge, which cannot be assigned to anyone. Your specific product,
your data, your business logic, and your designs stay with you.
