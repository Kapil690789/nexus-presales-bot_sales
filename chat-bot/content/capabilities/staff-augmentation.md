## What this covers

Engineers and designers who join your team, work in your stack, attend your standups,
and are managed by your engineering leadership. A monthly retainer rather than a scoped
project. Useful when you know what to build and need capacity, or need a specific skill
for a fixed period.

## How it differs from a project engagement

On a project, we own the outcome: we scope it, we run delivery, and we are accountable
for shipping it. On augmentation, you own the outcome and we supply the people. That
distinction matters more than it sounds, because it decides who is accountable when a
sprint slips.

If what you actually want is for someone else to own the delivery, a project engagement
is the right shape and usually the cheaper one. We will say so rather than sell you
seats.

## What we typically supply

Full-stack, frontend, backend, mobile engineers, product designers, and QA. A delivery
lead is included on engagements above a few people, because coordination stops being
free at that point.

Roles are matched to your existing stack. We do not ask you to change technology to
suit who is available.

## How it works in practice

- Minimum engagement of three months. Below that, the ramp-up consumes the value.
- One month's notice to end or change the shape of the engagement.
- People are named and interviewed by you before they start. No unnamed resource pools,
  and no substitutions without your agreement.
- They work your hours with a defined overlap window, in your tools, in your repository,
  under your code review process.
- Monthly review of what the engagement is delivering, with the option to change the
  mix.

## What this is not

- Not a recruitment service, and not a route to hiring the individual permanently.
- Not a fixed-scope commitment. Augmentation supplies capacity; it does not come with a
  delivery date, because we do not control the backlog or the priorities.
- Not a fix for an unclear product direction. Adding engineers to a team that does not
  know what to build makes the problem more expensive, not smaller. That situation
  usually wants a short discovery or design engagement first.
- Not a way to skip technical leadership. If nobody on your side is reviewing code and
  setting direction, embedded engineers will produce work nobody has an opinion about
  until it is too late.
