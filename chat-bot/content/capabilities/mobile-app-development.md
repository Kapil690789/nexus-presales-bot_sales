## What this covers

Consumer and business mobile products for iOS and Android: the app, the backend it
talks to, the integrations it needs, and getting it through app review. A typical
engagement runs from a discovery workshop to a first release in the stores, with a
named delivery lead throughout.

## The stacks we work in

- **Flutter** — our default for cross-platform work. One codebase, both platforms,
  consistent interface. Best fit when the app is flows, lists, and forms, which most
  business apps are.
- **React Native** — chosen when your team already writes React and will take the
  codebase over, or when you need to share logic with an existing web product.
- **Swift and Kotlin natively** — when the product depends on platform capability:
  serious background processing, deep hardware or sensor use, complex offline sync, or
  interfaces that need to feel unmistakably native.

Backends are typically Node.js or Python FastAPI with PostgreSQL, or Firebase when the
product is read-heavy and you would rather not operate infrastructure.

We recommend the stack after discovery, not before. Who maintains the app in two years
is usually a better guide than any technical comparison.

## What a first release usually looks like

One clearly-named user, one journey done properly, on the platforms that user actually
has. Authentication, the core loop, the two or three integrations that the loop cannot
work without, and analytics on the handful of events that tell you whether it is
working.

Things that commonly wait for a second release: in-app messaging, a second user role,
tablet layouts, offline mode, ratings and social features, and admin tooling beyond
what your team needs to run day one.

## What we include

Discovery and scoping, product design for the agreed release, engineering, QA on the
primary devices, store submission support, and handover documentation. Weekly demos
against working software rather than status decks.

## What this is not

- We do not put a marketing site or a brochure app in the stores.
- We do not do "convert our website into an app" with a wrapper. If a web view is the
  right answer, we will say so, and it is usually a web project.
- We do not take on app rescues sight-unseen. If you have an existing codebase, we
  start with a paid technical review before committing to a delivery scope, because
  the honest answer is sometimes that a rebuild costs less than the repair.
- We do not run your app indefinitely after launch as part of a build engagement.
  Ongoing support is a separate retainer.
