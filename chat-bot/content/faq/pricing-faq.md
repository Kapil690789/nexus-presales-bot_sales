## How is the price worked out?

From the service, the platforms, the integrations, and the features that change the shape
of the build — permissions, real-time behaviour, marketplace mechanics, AI features. Those
inputs produce an indicative range, which narrows to a firm proposal at the end of
discovery.

Deliberately not from a day rate multiplied by a guess at duration. What you are buying is
a scoped release, and the useful conversation is about what is in it.

## Why is it a range and not a price?

Because before discovery we cannot see your authentication constraints, the state of the
data you want to migrate, or which of your integrations behaves differently than its
documentation says. A single confident number at that point is either padded to cover what
we cannot see, or it is optimistic and becomes a change request later. The range says how
much is still unknown, and discovery closes it.

## Is the number I get from this assistant a quote?

No. It is an indicative range for the release described in the conversation, and it is
explicitly not a contractual quote. A strategist confirms scope, commercial terms, and
timeline on the consultation, and the proposal after discovery is the real number.

## What is included?

Discovery, design for the agreed release, engineering, QA on your primary devices or
browsers, and the weekly delivery cadence with a named lead. Every estimate also comes
with its exclusions listed — the usual ones are third-party subscription fees, formal
security audits and penetration tests, post-launch retainers, and integrations discovered
after scope is agreed.

## The range is above our budget. What now?

We cut scope, not the price of the same scope. One platform instead of two, one user
journey instead of three, a manual process where automation can wait. You get a smaller
product that works properly rather than the whole thing built thinly, and there is a
second release to grow into.

If the gap is large enough that no useful version fits, we will tell you at the first
conversation instead of after a proposal.

## Will you discount?

Not on the same scope. A discount means either the price was wrong or the team gets
thinner, and you would find out which during delivery. Reducing scope to reach a number is
a real answer and we will do that work with you.

## Can we get a fixed price?

Once discovery is complete and scope is locked, yes. Before discovery, no — pricing a
scope nobody has examined means pricing the risk, which makes it more expensive than it
needs to be, and then every clarification turns into a contractual argument. Discovery is
a small fixed-price phase for exactly this reason: you can buy certainty about the big
number before committing to it.

## Do you have a minimum engagement?

Yes, and it is the reason we turn some work down. Below a certain size an engagement
cannot carry a proper discovery, a designer, an engineer, and QA, and what you would get
is worse than what a freelancer would give you for the same money. The assistant will tell
you if what you have described is below the floor.

For staff augmentation the minimum is three months, because below that the ramp-up
consumes the value.

## How does payment work?

Discovery is invoiced as its own fixed-price phase. Delivery is invoiced monthly against
progress, with terms set in the contract. No large up-front payment for the whole
engagement, and no payment held hostage against handover — you have the code and the
infrastructure throughout.

## Is design charged separately from engineering?

Not in a build engagement; design for the agreed release is part of it. Design is also
available as a standalone engagement — research, prototype, or a redesign to move a
specific metric — which is priced on its own and is often the cheapest useful thing we
sell.

## What about ongoing costs after launch?

Two kinds, both yours rather than ours. Infrastructure and third-party services —
hosting, authentication provider, payment processing, model API usage for an AI product —
are billed to your accounts directly, and we size them during discovery so there is no
surprise. Then, optionally, a support or feature retainer with us. Neither is included in
the build estimate.

## Does the estimate change if we add scope mid-project?

Only by agreement, and only in writing. New scope either replaces something in the
release, extends the timeline, or extends the budget, and we put those three options in
front of you rather than absorbing the work quietly and letting the date slip. Trading
something of similar size in or out of the release costs nothing and happens constantly —
that is the point of working iteratively.
