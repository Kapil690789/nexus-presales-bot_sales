## How do we get started?

A consultation, then a short discovery phase. The consultation is a conversation with a
strategist about what you are building and whether we are a fit — no charge and no
obligation. If it makes sense on both sides, discovery is the first paid phase and it
produces the scope, the plan, and a firm proposal.

## How long does discovery take?

Usually one to two weeks of elapsed time, driven mostly by how quickly we can get your
stakeholders into a room together. Two or three workshop sessions, a technical review,
and then the documents.

## How soon can you start?

Start dates depend on current commitments and we will tell you honestly what is
available rather than saying "immediately" to be agreeable. Discovery can usually begin
sooner than delivery, and starting there means the ramp-up happens while scope is still
being settled.

## Who will actually work on my project?

A named delivery lead who stays for the whole engagement, plus the specialists the work
needs — designers, engineers, QA. You meet them before they start and they are not
swapped out without your agreement. The people in the consultation tell you exactly who
would be on the team.

## Can we talk to a past client?

Yes, where that client has agreed to act as a reference. We ask them first and we do not
volunteer a client's name until they have said yes, which is the same protection we
extend to you.

## Do you sign NDAs?

Yes, and we will work from your template rather than insisting on ours. Confidentiality
obligations survive the end of the engagement.

## Do we own the code?

Yes — all intellectual property in the deliverables transfers to you on payment. Open
source dependencies keep their own licences, and any of our internal tooling that ends up
in your codebase comes with a perpetual royalty-free licence, so you can take the product
to any other supplier without asking us.

## What if we already have a codebase?

We start with a paid technical review before committing to a delivery scope. Taking over
unfamiliar code sight-unseen is how both sides get surprised. The review sometimes
concludes that a rebuild is cheaper than the repair, and we would rather say that at the
start than discover it in month three.

## Can you work with our in-house team?

Yes, in either shape: a project where we own delivery and coordinate with your team, or
staff augmentation where your leadership owns delivery and we supply people. The
distinction matters because it decides who is accountable when a sprint slips, so we
settle it explicitly rather than leaving it fuzzy.

## What happens after launch?

You get a support window for defects in what we built, plus handover documentation and a
walkthrough so your team can run the product. Ongoing feature development is a separate
retainer, which we say up front — plenty of clients take the codebase over entirely, and
that is a normal, planned outcome rather than a failure.

## What do you need from us during the project?

A decision-maker who can settle scope questions within a day or two, access to the
systems we have to integrate with, and someone available for the demo every two weeks.
Projects slow down on waiting for a credential or a review far more often than on
technical difficulty, so your dependencies are tracked on the same board as ours.

## What if it is not working out?

Contracts have a notice period and a defined exit: you keep everything produced to that
point, in your repository, in your accounts, with documentation for what exists. There is
no lock-in that makes leaving expensive. Nobody plans for this, and the absence of a trap
is part of why the working relationship stays honest.

## Do you work with our timezone?

We work across the US, UK, EU, and India, with a defined overlap window agreed at the
start rather than assumed. The delivery lead is available in your working hours.

## What kinds of projects do you turn down?

Brochure and marketing-only websites, Shopify or WordPress theme work, crypto and web3
products, student or homework projects, and anything below our engagement minimum. We say
so at the first conversation and point you somewhere better rather than taking work we are
the wrong shape for.
